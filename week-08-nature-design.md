# Summary

**In a nutshell:** Nature is a system that has already solved many design problems. Understand the relationships, find the function, and translate it into human design.

## Systems thinking
- **Elements → relationships → patterns → behaviour.**
- Identify the nodes first, then the relationships between them. Think of relationships as *verbs*.
- **Ask of each relationship:** who benefits, what does each element give and take, and what goes in and out of the system?
- Compare how elements behave individually and collectively.
- **Leverage points** are places in a system where you can intervene to shift it.
- If you can simulate a system's core conditions, what emerges from them becomes fairly "obvious".
- **Applied to our project:** what does nature do when we discard a diaper, and what impact do its materials have?

## Observing nature
- What do we see, and not see? What do we think is happening? What lives there? What would we like to understand, such as how organisms manage with little water?
- **Forest bathing**, from Japan: time in nature as therapy.

## Biomimicry
- **Bio + mimicry:** to imitate life.
- **Three parts:** intention (ethos), effect (reconnect), act (emulate).
- **Three levels:** form, process and system.
- **Design question:** *what does nature [function] in [context]?*
- **Method:** biological function → abstracted function → design opportunity.
- Be inspired and translate; there's no need to copy directly.
- **Examples:** Velcro, ceramics learning from how seashells form, camels regulating temperature.

## Takeaway
> Nature doesn't have a design problem, people do. (William McDonough)
