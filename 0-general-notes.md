# General notes that are generic

An exercise to do to figure out - what a designer should do

1. What do designers do? -> follow up: suppose AI can start doing these things better than humans, what happens?
2. Why is design valuable?
3. Where is the money coming from?

-----

## Q1: What do designers (interaction designers) really do?

I asked AI, what does an interaction designer really do? It boiled down to some objective tasks that they particularly do. In fact, it reminded me of CIID's mantra

> Listen, build, test & repeat

1. Listen - to understand the problem deeper and clearly, sometimes to discover not-so-obvious things (also observe & document etc.)
2. Build - prototype in order to test and evaulate, make from the information at hand
3. Test - Evaluate and gather feedback. Almost like listen and observe again


## Q2: Why is design valuable?

1. IDEO founders have proven and shown impact of how listening and observing before working on your assumptions prove to be impactful. There's enough reason to believe that design is a required step in the process of building. Good design companies have long lasting effect of others continuing to use it. See Dieter Rams work and how it affected Braun and eventually influencing Johny Ive later to desing the iPod. Same with Xerox Parc and today, even AirBnB and many design led companies. In many ways, it's valuable because it (in)directly affects the profit. A one without design 100% is affected by less profits i.e. the reverse must be true. These are all somewhat, an external application determing the value of design

2. A more internal application, on the self - is it builds some problem solving capabilities, understanding and achieving clarity for problem at hand or even utilising tools and methods that are trasnferrable. These are some generalisable skills to approaching problem solving using creativity.

3. Lastly, there's this intangible aspect of the value of design - which is just pure joy. Something designed well, just feels good and joyful to use and play with. In addition, the joy of building, making and iterating to make something better is purely satisfying

## Q3: Where does the money come from?

- private / public funding for problem solving (large orgs to startups) - largely the funding for applications of design lies in organisations
- design education - mostly the point 2. from above end up here, for their satisfaction of designing and extending the process/methods of 'designing'

Lastly, the ROI can also be mesaured in how people graduating in studying design, even if ended up in non-design careers still make an impact somewhere by the application of it's methods.

## 🚨 What if AI gets good at various parts of the design process?

1. **What if AI better at "listening, building and testing"?**
The perception is that AI models at a instance in time, can read data, collate and analyse better. But in reality, the struggle is what data to track, what adds value to the customers/end-users and where does the investment of design make a difference? Organisations like SAP is having a hard time making sense of data gathered, even after putting it through models and frameworks. It's not adding 'value' directly. There's still human intervention required to make this more sophisticated.

Almost all of the value that is driven by design is at it's application level i.e. to identify and act on what really adds value to the user (of this well designed product)

2. **How does AI affect me (personally) build the problem solving muscle?**

Whether having AI or not having AI, me personally doing design - will still help me build that muscle internally. Analogy = me going to the gym will still build that muscle for me vs it doesnt' matter how much more weight the AI can lift (probably more, but the right scale of comparison)

3. **For the personal joy and satisfaction**
AI here actually has a negative effect - it sucks the joy and fun of making, building of it. For the optimist who really enjoys the building and making, when doing that activity, I am free to conclude on whatever comes out of it. In addition, I 'figure out' what works and what doesn't at the same time also assessing, what's possible with the resources available and lastly, boiling it down to it's simplicity and it's functionality. It's essentially, theory building - the set of systematic processes of generating these steps to figure out why a design would work (vs not work). 

4. **In addition...**

In fact the actual value of design is trying to arrive at a conclusion yourself and not something that someone else has given or AI has spat out. There might be incentives for AI companies that their model is now able to 'design' or generate 'designs' - which is good, but to the person, it's one of the options. Yes, it helps in automating some arduous tasks, but still doesn't substitute me becoming better at figuring out what to design.

But what if, assuming AI becomes better at 'figuring out' (point 2 in Q2) as well? This would mean, first is to maybe conceptualise a novel method of understanding the problem, conduct the actual research and gather new and relevant data and then perform the analysis and then design the most optimal solution that scales. It would still seem, even in this futuristic hypothetical scenario, that a human designer would still be required to intervene at the application level and understand how to apply to the right set of challenges? To ask - can this be applied to cure cancer? can this be applied to time travel? etc. Of course, this hypotheses still doesn't seem relevant for people who are studying design to build the design thinking muscle.

To be honest, there's a difference between a great designer and not so great designer. The great designer did build that muscle and figuring out the novel methods and processes to apply to challenging problems. The not-so-great designer still doesn't have that muscle - so the AI is not really adding value here. Also, there's fun in being in the community of designers who think alike - AI just takes away that too.

AI will still plausibly be unable to substitute the human joy of making - because the reason why someone is a designer is probably for the 'pure joy' of making. The community of designers and the like minded people who enjoy doing this, shall still exist. In that way, teaaching and learning from humans is unsubstituable by AI.

## So what now?

The emphasis of design should be more focused on 'figure it out yourself' aspects of it. Learning by doing method. 

So, in essence, if you are a designer or figuring out whether to study design - your fresh input is what is required in the field right now. The perception is important.
