# 🧰 The Toolkit

A toolbox and its manual. Built from weeks 1–8 at CIID, to open when I'm stuck on a real project.

**How to use it**
1. Find your situation in [Which tool do I grab?](#which-tool-do-i-grab)
2. Go to that card (search its code, e.g. `L2`).
3. For bigger jobs, use a ready-made [combo](#combos).

**Each card says:** 🎯 when to reach for it · 🔧 how to use it · 📍 where we used it · ⚠️ watch out

**How worn is the tool?**
- 🟢 used on a project
- 🟡 tried or applied in class, in an exercise or to a small case
- ⚪️ introduced, but my notes don't record using it yet
- ✏️ added by Claude: not from my notes

> "Where we used it" comes from my notes. Most "when to reach for it" lines and all the combos are Claude's ✏️ suggestions built on them, so change them as I learn more.

# Tray T: Team

### T1. Icebreaker chat 🟡
- 🎯 People meet for the first time, or a new person joins.
- 🔧 One-to-one, 3–4 mins each: where are you from? how did you get here? hobbies? **what do we have in common?**
- 📍 [Week 1](week-01-team-building.md): talking to Guglielmo, we found visualisation, visual programming and Python in common.

### T2. Culture scale 🟡
- 🎯 Working styles differ, and nobody has said so.
- 🔧 Ask a set of questions (we had 20). Everyone places themselves on a scale. Talk about the gaps.
- 📍 [Week 1](week-01-team-building.md): showed who prefers working in silos and who prefers teams. Ref: Erin Meyer, *The Culture Map*.

### T3. Team contract 🟢
- 🎯 Before any project work. Reopen it when ideating, voting and giving feedback.
- 🔧 Copy and fill:
  ```
  Success looks like (3 measures we can check):
  What each of us wants out of this:
  Constraints:
  Likely friction:
  Who does what (strengths / what I want to learn):
  When we work, check in and give feedback:
  ```
- 📍 [Week 1](week-01-team-building.md), "transcend a human life" project: ≥100 years, time must visibly move, Momo must be able to experience it.
- 📍 [Weeks 5–6](week-05-06-programming.md): each of us said our takeaway (do something simple / extrapolate simplicity / let people experience it), with one constraint: no physical computing.
- ⚠️ In [week 4](week-04-intro-interaction-design.md), sharing personal contracts came up during feedback. Better to do it on day one.

### T4. Forming → storming → norming → performing ⚪️
- 🎯 The team feels off, and I want to know if that's normal.
- 🔧 Name the stage together. *Forming:* get in sync on hopes, fears, roles. *Storming:* conflict. *Norming:* trust, knowing each other's needs. *Performing:* in rhythm.
- 📍 Introduced in [week 1](week-01-team-building.md) (Bruce Tuckman). Looking back, the [week 4](week-04-intro-interaction-design.md) friction looks like storming ✏️.

### T5. Split the work 🟢
- 🎯 A small team with a short deadline.
- 🔧 List tasks in order, give each one an owner, agree on the handover points.
- 📍 [Week 1](week-01-team-building.md), wallet project: tasks were clearly divided and we didn't feel short of time.
- 📍 [Week 4](week-04-intro-interaction-design.md): we both did all tasks simultaneously, and it was exhausting. Splitting linearly would have worked better.

### T6. Micro-excursion 🟡
- 🎯 People in the team have skills that others don't know about.
- 🔧 One person gives a short tour of something they know well.
- 📍 [Weeks 5–6](week-05-06-programming.md): Dennis on Umfeld. We also did this at SAP.

# Tray L: Look & listen

### L1. Spot observation 🟡
- 🎯 Before designing. People don't observe themselves, they just do.
- 🔧 Pick a spot, stay, document. Look for **routines, friction, workarounds, interactions, space**. Note one thing that stood out, small details, sounds and senses. Photo and video count as data.
- 📍 [Week 3](week-03-video-ethnography.md): observation exercise. [Week 8](week-08-nature-design.md): the same eye on plants (what do we see? what do we *not* see? what do we think is happening?).
- 💡 Best question: *what workarounds are people using, and what do they reveal?*
- ⚠️ Don't think about solutions while observing.

### L2. Specific questions 🟢
- 🎯 Answers are too general to design from.
- 🔧 Anchor to a **time, action and person**. Ask what they *did*, not what they *would* do.

  | Vague | Specific |
  |---|---|
  | How do you feel about being remote? | What does a typical conversation with staff look like? |
  | Why can't you connect? | Have you tried a casual chat with another student? How did it go? |
  | How much would you use? | How much did you use yesterday? ✏️ |

- 📍 [Week 4](week-04-intro-interaction-design.md): "unable to connect at a personal level" was too broad. Jose: break it into smaller components. One artefact can solve one specific aspect.
- 📍 [Week 1](week-01-team-building.md): our feedback to "The weight of us" team, whose question carried an assumption.

### L3. Prompt guide 🟢
- 🎯 Any planned interview.
- 🔧 3–5 prompts, each with an **objective** next to it. Two ready orders:
  - *Person and place:* who are you → what do you enjoy, and why → show me around → a memorable piece or moment.
  - *Object:* what do you carry now → habits and activities → what you wish you could carry → your ideal.
- 📍 [Week 3](week-03-video-ethnography.md): Riddhi, designer and hobby potter, 4 prompts, 20–30 mins. [Week 1](week-01-team-building.md): the perfect wallet (the second order).
- ⚠️ Impromptu questions were hard to categorise later. Go in pairs: one moderates, one takes notes.

### L4. Shot list 🟢
- 🎯 Filming or photographing research.
- 🔧 Tick off: person talking · hands with material · portrait in context · wide shot of the space · close-up · people interacting · artefacts/shelves · sounds.
- 📍 [Week 3](week-03-video-ethnography.md): pottery studio. The portrait shot was left unticked, and the video became more about the space than the person.
- ⚠️ I kept audio running but broke the video, so editing was a pain. Check focus, ISO, light, tripod, orientation, background noise. Film quirks and personality.

### L5. Tagged transcript 🟢
- 🎯 Turning a long recording into a story or findings.
- 🔧 Write down key lines per question → ⭐️ the strong ones → add `<video: shot to show>` → write "my take" separately → cut → arrange into chapters.
- 📍 [Week 3](week-03-video-ethnography.md): four audio recordings became a six-chapter video. Process-heavy parts were marked as maybe not needed.

### L6. Desk research 🟢
- 🎯 New domain, or before meeting an expert.
- 🔧 Understand the domain → list assumptions and hypotheses → gather sources → write questions → narrow down → work with a researcher.
- 📍 [Week 7](week-07-data-design.md): Bergamo's circular economy. Group searching filled gaps and produced questions. Also met Beautiful Soup and pandas for data work.

### L7. Interview map 🟢
- 🎯 One rich stakeholder interview, a whole team to inform.
- 🔧 Everyone takes notes → put them on one shared map → pull out questions and gaps.
- 📍 [Week 7](week-07-data-design.md): the Anja and municipality interview. Includes a good closing prompt, the **magic wand**: *what would CIID focus on?*

### L8. Data object 🟡
- 🎯 Warming up a team, or when "data" feels abstract.
- 🔧 Pick one ordinary object. Ask who, what, when, why. Circle the questions worth chasing.
- 📍 [Week 7](week-07-data-design.md): a local Bergamo cheese. *How was it made? How old? Why circular?* "Data begins with our curiosity."

# Tray M: Make sense

### M1. Affinity map 🟡
- 🎯 Many responses, no pattern yet.
- 🔧 One note per point → group what feels similar → name each group → look for outliers too.
- 📍 [Week 4](week-04-intro-interaction-design.md): clustering responses about being remote.

### M2. Insight ladder 🟡
- 🎯 I have clusters and need a "so what".
- 🔧 Climb it, with the illustrative example (not real findings) from [week 4](week-04-intro-interaction-design.md):
  1. **Observation:** a remote student learns of decisions when someone says "as we discussed".
  2. **Pattern:** four of seven learn about changes only after the fact.
  3. **Interpretation:** they don't know what they don't know, so they can't ask.
  4. **Insight:** pattern + why + a tension, opening a design question.
     ✏️ *e.g. Remote students depend on asking to stay included, but the gaps that matter most are the ones they can't see to ask about.*
- 📍 [Week 4](week-04-intro-interaction-design.md): the remote student brief.

### M3. Insight test ⚪️
- 🎯 Before building anything on an insight.
- 🔧 Check:
  - [ ] 3+ people and 2+ clusters (else it's a hunch)
  - [ ] Surprises a teammate
  - [ ] Breaks if I swap in another group
  - [ ] Names a tension
  - [ ] Contains no solution ("so what", not "so build this")
- 📍 Written up in [week 4](week-04-intro-interaction-design.md).

### M4. 5 Whys ⚪️
- 🎯 Data or a request points at a symptom.
- 🔧 Ask why, then why to the answer, about five times. Stop where you can act.
- 📍 Introduced in [week 7](week-07-data-design.md), with the reminder that correlation isn't causation.

### M5. Meaning behind the action ⚪️
- 🎯 A behaviour looks odd or irrational.
- 🔧 Two columns: *what they did* / *what it means to them*.
- 📍 My reading in [week 3](week-03-video-ethnography.md): Geertz's Balinese cockfight, which reminded me of the men jumping at Masai Mara.

### M6. System map 🟡
- 🎯 Many actors, where fixing one thing shifts another.
- 🔧 Nodes first → arrows as **verbs** → for each: who benefits? what is given and taken? → what goes in and out? → patterns → behaviour → **leverage points**, where a nudge shifts the whole.
- 📍 [Week 8](week-08-nature-design.md): connections between elements of nature. Then the question for our project: what does nature do when we discard a diaper?

# Tray F: Frame

### F1. How might we 🟡
- 🎯 After an insight, before ideas.
- 🔧 `How might we (action) for (people) in (context) that (goal)`. **Action and goal are musts.** Open up the noun: ❌ a new coffee *cup* → ✅ a new coffee *experience*.
- 📍 [Week 1](week-01-team-building.md): the coffee example. [Week 4](week-04-intro-interaction-design.md): the template.

### F2. Rewrite the brief 🟡
- 🎯 The brief is dull, too wide, or everyone will make the same thing.
- 🔧 What does it ask? What does it assume? What's its attitude? Rewrite it as something that matters to you, then check you still honour the constraints.
- 📍 [Weeks 5–6](week-05-06-programming.md): the SMALL brief (weird, cute, absurd, specific, away from laptop and keyboard). We added our own constraint.

### F3. Ask nature ⚪️
- 🎯 The obvious solutions are exhausted, or materials and resources matter.
- 🔧 `What does nature [function] in [context]?` → biological function → abstract it → design opportunity. Look at three levels: form, process, system. Translate, don't copy.
- 📍 [Week 8](week-08-nature-design.md): how camels regulate temperature, ceramics from how seashells form, Velcro. In [week 2](week-02-intro-labs.md) a weaver bird's nest had already inspired our bird feeder 🟢.

# Tray I: Ideas

**House rules:** be visual · go for quantity · stay on topic · keep the contract visible.

### I1. Crazy eights ⚪️
- 🎯 Need breadth fast, or to stop overthinking.
- 🔧 8 ideas in 8 minutes, drawn, alone. Then share.
- 📍 Taught in [week 1](week-01-team-building.md). My drawing is a growth area, so it's good practice.

### I2. Round robin / improv ⚪️
- 🎯 Loud voices dominate, or people cling to their own ideas.
- 🔧 *Round robin:* idea → pass → next person adds → pass again. *Improv:* build ideas through discussion.
- 📍 Taught in [week 1](week-01-team-building.md). We struggled to let go of personal ideas there, which is what round robin helps with ✏️.

### I3. Combination grid 🟢
- 🎯 The space is too open.
- 🔧 Pick two dimensions, list options on each, cross them:

  | | obstacle course | quiz | puzzle | improv |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | **face** | | | | |
  | **hands** | | | | |
  | **voice** | | | | |
  | **body** | | | | |

- 📍 [Weeks 5–6](week-05-06-programming.md): interaction × game. Ideas included driving a car with your face, voice pitch games and dodgeball.

### I4. Act it out (bodystorming) 🟡
- 🎯 Physical or spatial interactions, or when talking stalls.
- 🔧 Ask "what all can I do with my body?" Perform the interaction with whatever is around. Note what you didn't foresee.
- 📍 [Week 4](week-04-intro-interaction-design.md): bodystorming in the Atrium. [Weeks 5–6](week-05-06-programming.md): acting out revealed unplanned points.

### I5. Four players 🟡
- 🎯 The group is stuck.
- 🔧 Who is being the **mover, follower, opposer, bystander**? Spot the missing one and play it.
- 📍 [Week 1](week-01-team-building.md): used as a lens in reflection (Siddhant "mover" + "opposer", Govind "more mover"). We agreed to use it more consciously. (David Kantor)

### I6. Ask nature
- See `F3`. It works for framing and for ideas.

# Tray D: Decide

### D1. Dot vote 🟢
- 🎯 Too many ideas, no appetite for debate.
- 🔧 Choose a lens → vote silently → talk about the top few. Lenses: most effective, most fun to prototype, most human-centred, most time/cost effective. **Best lens: the success measures in `T3`.**
- 📍 [Week 1](week-01-team-building.md): "transcend a human life" project.
- ⚠️ Let go of your own idea once it's out. Ours showed in the narration.

### D2. 2×2 matrix 🟢
- 🎯 A close vote, or a decision to justify to others.
- 🔧 Two axes that matter *here* (impact × effort is the default). Place, then discuss.
- 📍 [Week 1](week-01-team-building.md): listed with dot voting for the same project (axes not recorded).

# Tray X: Interaction

### X1. Do, feel, know 🟡
- 🎯 Starting or critiquing any interaction.
- 🔧 Choose a side for each, and say why:

  | Question | One end | Other end |
  |---|---|---|
  | How do you **do**? | Button: binary | Handle: continuous control |
  | How do you **feel**? | Cool: open, invites interpretation | Hot: direct, explicit |
  | How do you **know**? | Map: chart your own route | Path: follow the steps |

- 💡 Cool to attract, hot to inform. Paths for repetitive workflows, maps for exploring.
- 📍 [Week 4](week-04-intro-interaction-design.md) (Bill Verplank).

### X2. Verplank's board 🟢
- 🎯 After the HMW, to turn it into a design.
- 🔧 Fill the boxes:

  | Motivation | Meaning | Models | Mappings |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | **Idea** | **Metaphor** (prefer verbs) | **Model** | **Display** |
  | **Error** | **Scenario** | **Task** | **Control** |

- 📍 [Week 4](week-04-intro-interaction-design.md): the remote student brief, with notes in Figma. [The board](assets/w3-d2-image.png).
- ⚠️ Stay in the framework until it's filled. New directions mid-way were exhausting.

### X3. Loop and frictions ⚪️
- 🎯 People drop off, get confused or lose interest.
- 🔧 Trace **trigger → action → feedback → reward → progress**. At each step, is the friction *cognitive* (unclear), *operational* (slow, hidden, buggy) or *emotional* (no reward or visible progress)?
- 📍 Introduced in [week 4](week-04-intro-interaction-design.md).

### X4. Norman's checklist ⚪️
- 🎯 A quick review of any screen or object.
- 🔧 Discoverability · feedback · mental model · affordance · signifier · mapping · constraints ✏️
- 📍 Introduced in [week 4](week-04-intro-interaction-design.md). Example: a digital pen you flip to erase, like a pencil.

### X5. Laban's movement words ⚪️
- 🎯 Gestures, animation, physical movement, or explaining why motion "feels wrong".
- 🔧 Describe it, then shift one: **weight** (light ↔ strong), **space** (direct ↔ indirect), **time** (sudden ↔ sustained), **flow** (free ↔ bound) ✏️
- 📍 Introduced in [week 4](week-04-intro-interaction-design.md). My idea to try: naming easing curves this way.

### X6. Storyboard and flow ⚪️
- 🎯 Explaining an interaction, or handling many states.
- 🔧 *Storyboard:* frames of a person using it. *Flow:* steps and states, including errors ✏️.
- 📍 Introduced in [week 4](week-04-intro-interaction-design.md).

# Tray P: Prototype

### P1. Compass + timebox 🟡
- 🎯 Stuck, unfocused, near a deadline, or needing momentum.
- 🔧 `We're making this to find out: ____` (compass) + `We stop at: ____` (timebox). Use the least time and resources. If nobody can use it, it's a model, not a prototype.
- 📍 [Week 1](week-01-team-building.md): 8 switches with 2 states in 20 mins. [Week 2](week-02-intro-labs.md): timeboxed brainstorm for the bird feeder 🟢. [Week 4](week-04-intro-interaction-design.md): the compass idea.

### P2. Scrappy first version 🟢
- 🎯 An idea exists only in words.
- 🔧 Use what's already around. Whatever exists is part of the prototype. "I am here to make mistakes."
- 📍 [Week 2](week-02-intro-labs.md): the first feeder of stacked plates gave us the form and showed us what would be too hard.

### P3. Simplify to build 🟢
- 🎯 The design exceeds our skills, machines, time or assembly.
- 🔧 Get feedback straight after prototype one → list limits (skill, material, machine, assembly) → find the simplest form that keeps the core idea → say aloud what you're giving up.
- 📍 [Week 2](week-02-intro-labs.md): an organic nest became a hexagon with fewer plates, three repeated parts, scaled to the print bed. It lost some likeness and kept the idea.
- 💡 Building and assembly *are* design.

### P4. Spike 🟡
- 🎯 One risky technical unknown.
- 🔧 Timebox a tiny code test of only that unknown. Keep the answer, throw away the code ✏️.
- 📍 [Weeks 5–6](week-05-06-programming.md). Parts shelf: MediaPipe and OpenCV (face, hands, body), Keystone (projection mapping), p5/Processing, OSC (device to device).

### P5. Small chunks (with or without AI) 🟢
- 🎯 Building something in code.
- 🔧 Bare minimum first → one isolated piece at a time. I decide the library, approach and functions; AI does the nitty-gritty.
- 📍 [Weeks 5–6](week-05-06-programming.md): the gesture and dodgeball game, then enhancements (sound, graphics, 3 lives).
- ⚠️ Ask how connected I still feel to the result.

### P6. Human computer 🟡
- 🎯 Testing instructions, logic or onboarding before building.
- 🔧 One person gives instructions, another follows them literally. Fix where it breaks.
- 📍 [Weeks 5–6](week-05-06-programming.md): drawing a flower on a 3×3 grid from instructions.

### P7. Wizard of Oz ⚪️
- 🎯 Testing a smart system that doesn't exist yet.
- 🔧 A hidden person plays the system; watch how people respond. Video tricks and stop motion also work.
- 📍 Mentioned in [week 3](week-03-video-ethnography.md): controlling Netflix with gestures.

### P8. Fusion 360 moves 🟢
- 🎯 Modelling a physical part.
- 🔧 **Sketch in 2D first**, then: extrude (pull up) · revolve (spin, for circular) · fillet/chamfer (edges) · shell (hollow) · project (copy geometry) · section analysis (check joinery).
- 📍 [Week 2](week-02-intro-labs.md): bird feeder.

# Tray S: Share & feedback

### S1. Context → observation → impact → next ⚪️
- 🎯 One specific event, especially a difficult one.
- 🔧 `In (event), I saw (fact). It affected (team/work) by ____. Next time, ____.`
- 📍 Introduced in [week 1](week-01-team-building.md).

### S2. Start / stop / continue 🟢
- 🎯 End of a project or sprint.
- 🔧 Each person gives each teammate one or more of each. Make them actionable.
- 📍 [Week 1](week-01-team-building.md): wallet project. [Week 4](week-04-intro-interaction-design.md): both ways.
- 💡 Mine to keep reading: stop saying "it's fine", don't compete, go easier on myself, draw more. Keep: notes, good questions, synthesis.

### S3. Story chapters 🟢
- 🎯 Presenting people or research.
- 🔧 Arrive → meet the person → what drives them → how they do it → a memory → close. **Get to the point fast.**
- 📍 [Week 3](week-03-video-ethnography.md): Riddhi's video.

### S4. Process trail 🟢
- 🎯 Portfolio, reviews, stakeholders asking "why this?"
- 🔧 Questions → first sketch → prototype → what changed and why → final → learnings. Photograph people testing.
- 📍 [Week 2](week-02-intro-labs.md): bird feeder deck. [Weeks 5–6](week-05-06-programming.md): "documenting work is super important".

# Combos

Tools snapped together for real-life jobs ✏️

| Job | Sequence |
|---|---|
| **Kick off a project** | `T1` → `T3` → `T5` → `L6` |
| **A stakeholder hands me a solution** | `M4` → `L2` → `M2` → `F1` |
| **Research sprint** | `L6` → `L3` + `L4` → `L1` → `L5` → `M1` → `M2` → `M3` |
| **One-day concept** | `F1` → `I1` → `I2` → `D1` → `P1` → `P2` → `S2` |
| **Fix an existing product** | `L1` → `X3` → `X4` → `F1` → `P2` → test |
| **Design a new interaction** | `F1` → `X1` → `X2` → `I4` → `X6` → `P2` |
| **"We have this cool tech"** | `P4` → `I3` → `D1` → `P5` → `S4` |
| **Policy or sustainability problem** | `L6` → `L7` → `M6` → `M4` → `F3` → `F1` |
| **Physical product** | `F3` → sketch → `P2` → feedback → `P3` → `P8` |
| **Team is in trouble** | `T4` → `S1` → reopen `T3` → `T5` |

CIID's mantra is the master sequence: **Listen** (L, M, F) → **Build** (I, D, X, P) → **Test** (back to L) → **repeat**. T and S run throughout.
