### Final project ppt - notes


**Slide 3 - First sketches**
Before we started out - we asked ourselves, 
What could attract birds to perch at the feeder?
How can it support to feed 4 - 5 sparrow sized birds at a time?

We exercised a timeboxed brainstorming session where we came up with a few of these ideas. After gathering our thoughts, we aligned on a similar idea of a weaver bird shaped nest inspired feeder

**Slide 4 - First prototype**

Iteration 01
We initially thought of creating multiple (6 - 7) layers of plates with varying diameters - so as to help mimic the nest structure (slightly organic shaped)
This gave us the idea of the form
At this stage we started to think about materials and how we would build it (3D printed mesh honeycomb mesh and wooden plates by cutting wood)
At this stage we also needed to look at the tools and materials available around us
It was necessary and very helpful to get feedback at this stage - Jose mentioned + we realised later (skills) that modeling an organic sphere would be challenging

**Slide 5 - 2nd Sketch**
So we had a 2nd sketch after the first iteration + feedback
We modified the design to make it easier to model and print. This was crucial - it simplified by a large margin
We made it into a hexagonal shape instead of the natural shape

Compromise / Constraints
We had to reduce the number of plates from the previous iteration, considering the size of the hexagon opening + print plate size (180cm x 180cm x 180cm)
The hexagonal shape made it easy for repetitions and assembly


**slide 6 - 3D Model**
The model was simplified to 3 main shapes - top face, middle face and bottom face. We just repeated the printing process. The symmetrical design helped organise the printing process. We had to scale down the model for the print bed size which was 180 x 180 x 180 cm

- constraints
- assembly
- project on a plane (etc.)

**Learnings**
- Iterations weren’t linear

- Getting feedback after 1st prototype was crucial - to help modify our design according to capacity and resources available
Circular to hexagonal simplified the design and construction part - without compromising on parts of the original idea. The compromise was that the end form didn’t exactly match the shape of a bird’s nest. (Learning: compromised justice to original idea - but that’s alright)


- <BIG> - The compromises we made realising that modeling (fusion360) the original would be challenging based on existing capabilities and assembly structures (“design” is impacted by things that are beyond design - which means, even building and printing is part of ‘design’) as opposed to how organisations look at this separately

- Take assembly into consideration when designing
